Frequently Asked Questions

Find answers to common queries regarding automatic hand dryer technology, energy consumption, installation, and procurement.

An automatic hand dryer utilizes an infrared (IR) optical sensor beam. When hands enter the detection zone (typically 10-15 cm below or inside the chamber), the optical reflection triggers an internal microchip relay that instantly fires the high-speed brushless turbine motor. High-velocity air is blown across hands to rapidly sweep droplets away. When hands are withdrawn, the sensor deactivates within 0.5 seconds, saving electricity.

Modern high-speed hand dryers operate at between 800W and 1200W, and dry hands in just 7 to 10 seconds. In contrast, older conventional warm-air dryers ran at 2400W for 35 to 45 seconds. Because drying time is reduced by 75% and motor wattage is halved, our dryers consume up to 85% to 90% less electricity, costing less than $0.0005 per individual hand dry.

Traditional push-button dryers relied on high-wattage heating coils to evaporate moisture, taking 30 to 45 seconds. Our high-velocity jet air dryers utilize 90 to 105 m/s air speed to mechanically strip and shear water droplets off skin in just 7 to 10 seconds, preventing washroom queues and wet hands.
